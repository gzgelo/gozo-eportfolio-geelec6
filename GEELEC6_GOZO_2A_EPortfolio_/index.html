01 / Course Experience

The Journey Through
Reading Visual Arts

A Semester of Growth

Enrolling in GEELEC 6 — Reading Visual Arts — opened my eyes to how deeply technology is woven into every fabric of modern existence. Before this course, I perceived technology primarily as a set of tools. After each lecture, reflection, and discussion, I came to understand it as a dynamic social, ethical, and cultural force that shapes who we are and how we relate to one another.

The subject challenged me to think beyond code and algorithms. It pushed me to ask harder questions: Who benefits from these systems? Who is left behind? What responsibilities do we carry as future IT professionals entering a world already saturated with digital dependencies?

Challenges I Overcame

The most significant challenge I faced was bridging the gap between abstract technological concepts and their real-world implications. Discussions on digital ethics, the digital divide, and data privacy forced me to confront uncomfortable truths — that the very technologies I aspire to build can also exclude, manipulate, or harm.

Writing the reflection paper on the documentary was particularly demanding. It required me to synthesize personal observation with academic critique — a skill I had not fully developed before this course. Learning to be both analytical and empathetic in my writing was a transformative experience.

Technical & Intellectual Takeaways

Beyond its humanistic dimensions, the course reinforced critical technical literacy. I gained a deeper appreciation for how networks, platforms, and data systems operate not just mechanically, but politically and socially. The ability to read a documentary with an IT lens, participate in group academic discourse, and produce structured academic outputs has sharpened my competence as both a student and a future professional.

I leave this course not just with grades, but with a richer, more nuanced worldview — one that will serve as the ethical foundation for every system I will eventually design, build, and deploy.

"

Technology is not neutral. It carries the values, biases, and intentions of those who build it. This course taught me that being a good IT professional means being a responsible human being first.

Angelo Gozo, BSIT 2-A

Collaborative Academic Discourse

This discussion paper represents one of the most intellectually stimulating collaborative efforts of the semester. As part of Group 1, we were tasked with synthesizing our collective understanding of a critical theme in the course — examining how living in the Reading Visual Arts reshapes identity, community, and responsibility.

The process of writing collaboratively was as educational as the content itself. We had to negotiate perspectives, reconcile conflicting interpretations, and produce a unified voice from multiple individual experiences. It required active listening, intellectual flexibility, and a shared commitment to academic rigor.

The finished paper stands as a testament to what collaborative scholarship can achieve — a document that is richer, more multifaceted, and more insightful than any one of us could have produced alone.

Course Instructor

Jhonna D. Alcantara

GEELEC 6 — Reading Visual Arts

A Heartfelt Reflection to My Instructor

From the very first day of class, it was clear that this course would be unlike any I had taken before — and that was entirely by design. The way our instructor approached each topic with a blend of intellectual rigor and genuine human warmth set the tone for a learning environment that felt both challenging and safe.

What I am most grateful for is how you never let us settle for surface-level answers. Every quiz, every discussion prompt, every reflective task was engineered to push us past what we already knew and into the discomfort of what we had yet to understand. You taught me that discomfort, when embraced with curiosity, is the birthplace of genuine learning.

The documentary screening and subsequent reflection assignment was, for me, a turning point. The way you framed the discussion — connecting the film's narrative to the broader social implications of technology — helped me see that IT is not merely an engineering discipline. It is a humanistic one, with the power to shape lives, communities, and futures. That perspective has permanently altered how I think about my own educational and professional path.

The group discussion paper was another milestone I will carry forward. You structured the collaborative process in a way that required us to listen to each other, challenge each other respectfully, and arrive at shared understanding without surrendering our individual voices. That skill — productive intellectual collaboration — is one I will use long after this semester ends.

To my instructor: thank you for seeing more in us than we saw in ourselves. Thank you for the patience with which you answered questions, the care with which you gave feedback, and the passion with which you taught. You did not merely transfer knowledge — you ignited a lifelong habit of asking the right questions.

I carry this course — and your guidance — as one of the most valuable chapters of my academic formation. I am a better thinker, a more empathetic technologist, and a more thoughtful person because of it.

Angelo Gozo BSIT 2-A • GEELEC 6
